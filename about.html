A LITTLE PHOTOBOOTH HISTORY

A century of stepping in and striking a pose.

Photo booths made portrait photography spontaneous and personal. Their format changed over time, but the fun of making a picture together stayed.

  1. 1889

    An early photo machine

    French inventor T. E. Enjalbert presented an early photography machine at the Paris World's Fair. It was a forerunner to the self-service booths that came later.

  2. 1925

    The Photomaton arrives

    Anatol Josepho's modern photo booth opened near Times Square. Customers could make a series of portraits by themselves, without a photographer.

  3. 1950s–60s

    A familiar place to pose

    Booths became a common sight at fairs, shopping centers, and train stations. Friends, couples, and families made small photo strips to keep and share.

  4. Today

    A classic idea, in new forms

    Digital cameras and phones brought photo effects and instant sharing into the experience. Vintage booths are still loved, while new digital versions carry the tradition forward.

History sources: Getty Museum and The Photographers’ Gallery.
